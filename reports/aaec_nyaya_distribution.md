## 5. Why AAEC strongly favors Anumana

### Genre and discourse structure

The AAEC corpus consists of **argumentative student essays** decomposed into MajorClaims, Claims, and Premises. This genre is inherently **inferential**: writers defend positions, draw consequences, and chain reasons together. Even when explicit markers like *because* or *therefore* are absent, the rhetorical function of most spans is still **reason-giving** rather than direct perception, testimony, or analogy.

### Component-type skew

- **Premises** dominate the extracted pool (500 of 1500 in the annotation sheet sample) and function as supporting reasons — a natural fit for Anumana.
- **MajorClaims** state positions to be argued, not sensory observations.
- **Claims** are intermediate argumentative moves, again typically inferential.

### Lexical cue scarcity

Only **2** rows (0.1%) contain Pratyaksha cues (observation, measurement, data, records, survey results). **17** rows (1.1%) contain Shabda cues (according to, experts, studies, authorities). **52** rows (3.5%) contain Upamana cues (like, similar to, analogous to, comparison).

AAEC essays rarely cite primary data, field measurements, or formal authorities in the short spans extracted; they more often use general reasoning and opinion.

### Heuristic default effect

**1408** rows (93.9%) received Anumana because **no pramana cue matched**. The default reflects the dominant argumentative function but amplifies the Anumana skew; many of these may be weak Anumana or unlabeled mixed rhetoric requiring human review.

### False-positive Upamana from "like"

Several Upamana assignments come from the substring **"like"** in non-analogical contexts (e.g., *would like to*, *is like anyone*). AAEC under-represents true Upamana (explicit comparison/analogy) while the heuristic over-triggers on common English function words.

## 7. Recommendations for collecting missing classes

### Pratyaksha (direct perception / empirical observation)

- **Science lab reports** and field notes with explicit measurements, readings, and observations.
- **News eyewitness** segments and citizen journalism with sensory verbs (*I saw, we measured, the sensor showed*).
- **Medical/clinical charts** summarizing vitals, imaging, and examination findings.
- **Survey methodology** excerpts reporting raw response counts and data tables.
- **Product reviews** grounded in hands-on use (*after testing for two weeks, the battery lasted…*).

### Shabda (testimony / authoritative word)

- **Academic abstracts** with citation frames (*Smith et al. (2023) report that…*).
- **Policy briefs** referencing government guidelines, WHO/CDC advisories, or legal statutes.
- **Journalism** using *according to officials / researchers / the ministry*.
- **Textbook** and encyclopedia definitional passages.
- **Expert interview** transcripts with attributed claims.

### Upamana (analogy / comparison)

- **Op-ed analogies** with explicit *just as / similar to / analogous to* structures.
- **Science popularization** comparing unfamiliar phenomena to familiar ones.
- **Legal reasoning** precedents (*this case is like Brown v. Board in that…*).
- **Debate** corpus arguments using comparative metaphors deliberately.
- Filter for **multi-word analogy cues** to avoid false hits on *would like to*.

### Anumana (retain but calibrate)

- Keep AAEC as a strong **inferential backbone** but cap its share in training merges.
- Use `aaec_review_priority.csv` to manually validate non-default Anumana and all non-Anumana candidates before merging into `real_world_dataset.csv`.
- Combine AAEC with **balanced external slices** (target ≥50–100 examples per pramana per source).
