## Interpretation

### Why raw TTR alone is misleading

- **Synthetic TTR** ≈ `0.0034` with **108,107** tokens — the enormous token mass drives TTR toward zero even though the vocabulary is large (364 types).
- **Real-world TTR** ≈ `0.5182` on only **2,603** tokens — a much smaller corpus yields a higher ratio by construction (fewer repeated types per token).
- TTR is **inversely correlated with corpus length**: comparing TTR across corpora of different sizes confounds lexical richness with sample size.

### Complementary indices

- **Guiraud's R** (0.783 synthetic vs 18.696 real-world) partially normalizes for length via √N in the denominator.
- **MATTR (window=50)** (0.797 vs 0.835) computes local TTR over fixed-length segments, reducing sensitivity to global repetition from template-heavy synthetic generation.

Together, these metrics show that the synthetic set is **template-dense** (high repetition at scale) while the real-world set is **lexically sparser per document** but drawn from more varied phrasing — supporting the professor's concern that synthetic diversity metrics must be reported with length-aware indices, not TTR alone.
